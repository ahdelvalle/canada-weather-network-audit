# Windrose — Canada Precipitation Network Reliability Report

**An independent, non-commercial research project assessing station-level reliability of Environment and Climate Change Canada's (ECCC) precipitation monitoring infrastructure.**

Published April 16, 2026. 64 pages. 12 provinces and territories.

---

## Key Findings

- **8,374 stations** in ECCC's inventory; **6,797 (81.2%) are decommissioned**
- Effective active network: **1,577 stations** across all of Canada
- **370 active stations (23.5%)** have confirmed transmission issues
- **143 stations** are hard blackouts — zero data transmitted
- **101 ghost stations** — nominally active, last data received before 2018
- **10,261,304 daily records** downloaded across 1,434 stations over 36 years
- Average data quality score across the network: **52.95%**
- **82% of 85 canonical reference city stations are decommissioned** — including
  Calgary, Edmonton, Vancouver, Winnipeg, Toronto, Halifax, and Regina
- **Zero EXCELLENT-rated stations** in the Northwest Territories or Nunavut
- Best-performing provinces: BC (65.9%), SK (64.1%), QC (63.7%)
- Worst-performing: Nunavut (31.0%), NWT (31.4%)

---

## Methodology

Each station was assessed on data availability, transmission continuity, and record
completeness over a 36-year observation window. The classification framework was developed
in accordance with criteria specified by the Office of Minister Julie Dabrusin
(Environment and Climate Change Canada) in January 2026.

Full methodology, station-level findings, and policy analysis are documented in the
complete report and the interactive dashboards at [windrose.news](https://windrose.news).
